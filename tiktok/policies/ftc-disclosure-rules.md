# FTC Disclosure — Owner/Operator Edition

You operate the TikTok Shop for 3rd Eye Supply as the brand owner's spouse / co-operator. That's a meaningfully different FTC posture than an arms-length affiliate or influencer. This file covers what you need to disclose, what you DON'T, and where the edge cases are.

**Primary FTC source:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers

## The shape of the rule

The FTC's "Endorsement Guides" require disclosure of "material connections" between an endorser and a brand. The rule is built for the case where a viewer might assume the endorser is independent when they're not.

For owner-operators (you), there's NO assumption of independence to correct — you ARE the brand. The disclosure has to be CLEAR but it doesn't have to repeat on every single post.

## The simple rule for you

For 3rd Eye Supply Brand Account content (Brand-Account-posted, Brand-Account-tagged, brand-relevant), you need:

✅ **One thing: a clear, persistent bio disclosure** that makes the brand-owner relationship obvious.

Acceptable bio disclosures:
- "Official TikTok of 3rd Eye Supply"
- "3rd Eye Supply | Owner-operated"
- "[Name], co-owner of 3rd Eye Supply"
- "Mike & [Wife's name] | 3rd Eye Supply"

That's it. No `#ad` on individual posts. No Branded Content toggle. The bio + the obvious brand-account identity satisfies "clear and conspicuous."

## The edge cases

Where disclosure DOES still matter:

### Edge case 1 — Posting to a PERSONAL account that doesn't carry the brand name

If you ever post 3rd Eye Supply content from your personal TikTok account (your own handle, your own profile, your own audience), the FTC's "material connection" rule kicks in because viewers might assume you're a regular customer recommending a product, not the owner's spouse.

In that case:
- `#ad` in caption first line (FTC standard)
- One-line in-bio note ("I co-own a small spiritual goods business") satisfies if persistent

Recommendation: don't mix personal and brand accounts. Pick the Brand Account and stay there.

### Edge case 2 — Promoting a DIFFERENT brand from the Brand Account

If you ever feature another brand's product on the 3rd Eye Supply Brand Account (a partner brand collab, a recommended tool you use, etc.), THAT post is an endorsement of an external party and requires:
- `#ad` in caption first line
- Branded Content toggle ON
- Disclose what your relationship is ("they sent us samples" / "we have an affiliate code")

Rare scenario, but worth knowing.

### Edge case 3 — Health-adjacent claims

The FTC enforces against false health claims independently of any disclosure rule. Disclosure doesn't fix a banned claim — saying "as the owner, I claim this cures anxiety" is just as banned as an influencer saying it.

See [voice-and-claims.md](voice-and-claims.md) for the never-say list. FTC health-claim enforcement is on top of everything else.

### Edge case 4 — Customer testimonials in your content

If you feature a customer comment ("Sarah said this helped her sleep") in a video or pin a customer's comment on a LIVE:
- The customer's claim is YOUR claim once you amplify it.
- If the customer's claim violates the never-say list (health, outcome, pseudoscience), you can't pin/share it even though it's their words.
- Always cross-check against [voice-and-claims.md](voice-and-claims.md) before pinning.

## What "clear and conspicuous" means for your bio

The bio disclosure has to actually be visible:

✅ At the top of your TikTok bio, NOT buried at the bottom
✅ Plain text, not hidden in emoji-only lines
✅ Visible without tapping "more" on profile views
✅ Present 100% of the time (don't remove it during content surges)

❌ "💎 ⚡ 🌙 owner" with no plain-text context
❌ Buried at the bottom of a long bio
❌ Only in a Story or pinned video — needs to be in the actual bio

## Health-claim enforcement is its own thing

The FTC will treat product-related health claims as the BRAND'S claims regardless of who said them. So a misleading claim made by you in a LIVE = a claim made by 3rd Eye Supply. The brand can be fined.

- Cannot claim a product treats, cures, prevents, or mitigates a disease.
- Cannot claim a product has specific physiological effects without scientific evidence.
- "Structure/function" claims (the lower bar for supplements) generally require evidence.

3rd Eye Supply products aren't supplements, drugs, or devices, so they shouldn't be making any of these claims to begin with. But the FTC will treat user-generated content (your TikToks) as part of the brand's marketing. If you make a health claim, the brand can be liable.

## What if I'm asked to recommend the products in DMs or email?

The same FTC logic applies. If you respond to a DM from a personal account ("yes, this incense is great") and the customer doesn't know you're the owner's spouse, that's an undisclosed material connection.

Easy fix: respond from the Brand Account, OR put your relationship in your DM signature ("- [Name], 3rd Eye Supply").

## TikTok Shop's own disclosure

Separate from the FTC, TikTok Shop ALSO has a disclosure mechanism — the auto-applied "Sponsored" / "Promoted" badge on tagged products. This is TikTok's signaling, not FTC compliance. The badge satisfies TikTok's rules but isn't sufficient for FTC alone (which is why your bio disclosure matters).

Both together = belt and suspenders. The bio disclosure does the FTC work; the TikTok badge does TikTok's work.

## Practical: what you actually need to do

For every video and LIVE on the Brand Account:

1. ✅ Confirm bio disclosure is in place (one-time setup; check monthly).
2. ✅ Use product tags via TikTok Shop's "Add product" flow.
3. ✅ Don't make health claims, outcome guarantees, or pseudoscience claims.
4. ✅ Use soft CTAs (see [/content-system/caption-formulas.md](../content-system/caption-formulas.md)).

That's the whole list. Four steps. No per-post hashtag discipline.

## Audit checklist (do once a month)

- [ ] Bio disclosure is still in place, visible without "more" tap
- [ ] No content posted from personal account that promotes 3rd Eye Supply without disclosure
- [ ] No external-brand endorsements on Brand Account without `#ad`
- [ ] No pinned/featured customer comments that make banned claims
- [ ] Reply to customer DMs from Brand Account (not personal)

30 seconds. Once a month. Done.

## What happens if FTC enforces

- The FTC goes after the BRAND (3rd Eye Supply / Mike) first, not individual creators. As owner-operator, that's effectively you and Mike.
- Fines historically: $50K-$250K per incident for small brands, much higher for repeat offenders.
- State-level versions (CA, NY) can compound federal action.
- The TikTok account itself may also be flagged independently.

The good news: bio-disclosure compliance is the easiest version of FTC compliance to maintain. Set once, audit monthly, move on.
