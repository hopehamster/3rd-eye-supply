# TikTok Hook Templates — 3rd Eye Supply

The first 1.5 seconds decide whether the video gets watched. These are battle-tested hook structures adapted to metaphysical/spiritual product content.

## The Five Hook Categories

### 1. Symptom-aware hook (best for cold traffic)

**Structure:** name a SPECIFIC daily annoyance + a second one nobody admits + reframe.

Template: *"If [specific symptom], and [second symptom nobody admits], you're not [wrong self-diagnosis]. You're [real reframe — which is what this product is for]."*

Examples:
- "If your apartment feels stale every Sunday, and three different candles haven't fixed it — you're not picky. You're trying to mark a transition that needs a different kind of cue." *(→ incense)*
- "If you've replaced the same crystal three times because you 'lose it' — you don't lose things. You're using it as a worry stone, and you should buy the kind that's meant to be carried." *(→ rings or polished stones)*
- "If you light incense, scroll for 20 minutes, then notice it burned out — that's not the incense's fault. That's the setup." *(→ incense + a ritual angle)*

Why it works: the audience is doing pattern-match on the symptom before they hear the product. Source: Martins, *Stand Out or Fade Away* — symptom positioning.

### 2. Tradition / explainer hook (best for higher-ticket)

**Structure:** name the tradition, name what people get wrong about it, deliver the real version.

Template: *"Everyone calls [product/material] 'a [generic thing]'. It's actually [specific tradition with provenance], traditionally used for [specific function]. Here's what changed when I started using it [correctly/intentionally]."*

Examples:
- "Everyone calls palo santo 'a candle scent.' It's actually a tree from Andean ceremony, traditionally burned in short pieces at the start of a quiet morning. Here's the difference between using it that way and using it like a Yankee Candle." *(→ if/when palo santo is added)*
- "Everyone treats clear quartz as 'the boring crystal.' Practitioners call it the master healer — not because it does anything dramatic, but because it's the one you use to amplify whatever else you're working with." *(→ Clear Quartz Pyramid)*

Why it works: positions you as a guide (StoryBrand — guide not hero), respects the audience's intelligence.

### 3. POV / morning-routine hook (best for entry-tier products)

**Structure:** "POV: you [specific scenario]" + show the product in use, no pitch.

Template: *"POV: [specific time/context] and you [specific action involving the product]."*

Examples:
- "POV: it's Sunday at 7am, you have the apartment to yourself for the first time this week, and you're about to light the first incense of fall."
- "POV: client just cancelled the third time, you light the creativity stick anyway, and try one more time."
- "POV: you finally bought the pyramid you've been saving for, and you're testing which window catches the morning light."

Why it works: no claim, no pitch, just inhabits a feeling. Audience supplies their own meaning.

### 4. Stitched / response hook (best for engagement)

**Structure:** Stitch a popular video in the niche (or a comment), then add genuine context.

Template: *"Stitching @user — yes, but here's the part everyone leaves out: [specific tradition/use/material detail]."*

Examples:
- Stitch a video that says "crystals are pseudoscience" → "Yes — and also, ritual objects have been part of every human culture for 30,000 years. The crystal isn't the medicine. The ritual is. The crystal is the cue."
- Stitch a "manifestation" video → "Yes, and also — be careful. 'Manifesting' as a marketing word covers a tradition that's about *attention*, not *outcomes*. What people are actually doing with these tools is paying attention. The outcomes follow because they paid attention, not because the object did the work."

Why it works: engagement multiplier (the stitched creator's audience sees you), demonstrates you're in the conversation, builds credibility through nuance.

⚠️ Be careful: never stitch in a way that mocks or dunks. The audience overlaps; your future buyers may follow the person you're stitching.

### 5. Demo / process hook (best for visual products: incense, pyramids)

**Structure:** show the product in a real moment of use, no voiceover for the first 2 seconds, then a single concise sentence.

Template: *[silent visual of product in use for 1.5-2s] → [one sentence over a continued visual] → [one sentence describing what it is + price] → CTA in caption.*

Examples:
- 2s of smoke curling from black love incense on a ceramic dish → "Black love incense is the one people light when they want the room to soften." → "Hand-rolled, $10, link in bio."
- 2s of morning light through golden quartz pyramid → "I keep this one in the window that gets the first sun." → "Golden quartz pyramid, $55."

Why it works: TikTok's algorithm weights watch-time. Silent opening forces sound-on engagement. The product IS the spectacle.

## Hook Don'ts

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| "Three things you need to know about [crystal]" | Listicle hooks are dead in 2026 — too commercial-feeling. |
| "You won't BELIEVE what this incense did..." | Sounds like a scam. Audience scrolls. |
| "Buy this if you want to manifest [outcome]" | TikTok pulls. FTC fines. (See policies/voice-and-claims.md) |
| Generic "Get ready with me" applied to crystals | The GRWM format works for makeup. For spiritual goods it reads as cosplay. |
| "Doctors HATE this one trick" | Auto-ban from spiritual / wellness adjacent. |
| Opening with the product on a white background like an Amazon listing | Looks like a paid ad. Algorithm deprioritizes. |

## Hook Quality Test (run before filming)

Read your hook out loud. Three questions:

1. **Specific?** "This incense is great for relaxation" → no. "If your apartment feels stale every Sunday" → yes.
2. **Earned?** Are you making a claim you couldn't defend if someone in the comments pushed back? Reframe with tradition/sensory/experience.
3. **One thing?** A hook can only do ONE job. Don't combine symptom + tradition + POV — pick one.

If any answer is "not quite", rewrite. Don't film a 3/5 hook to "see how it does" — TikTok will train its model on what you give it, and weak hooks teach it your account is mid.

## Combo / sequencing (the second video matters)

When you find a hook that hits (>10k views in 24h), the follow-up should NOT be a different hook on the same product. It should be:

- **Day 2**: same product, "Part 2 — the part I didn't fit in the first video"
- **Day 3**: same product, the comments-question they all asked
- **Day 5**: a *different* product the first audience would also want, hooked via "If you saved [first video], you'll want this too"

This is how a single winning video turns into 4-5 winning videos. Don't waste it by jumping to a different SKU on day 2.
