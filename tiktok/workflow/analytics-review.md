# Weekly Analytics Review (30 minutes, Fridays)

The Friday analytics block is what separates accounts that grow from accounts that just post. It's NOT about chasing virality — it's about identifying which hooks, products, and structures are EARNING attention, and doing more of those.

## Where to look

Open TikTok app → Profile → "Tools" (top-right hamburger) → "Analytics". Three tabs matter:

1. **Overview** — week-over-week trends
2. **Content** — per-video performance
3. **Followers** — audience composition

Web version: tiktok.com/tiktokstudio/analytics — easier for export.

## The 6 numbers that matter (weekly)

For each video posted in the last 7 days, log:

| Metric | What it tells you | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Total views** | Algorithmic reach | Trending UP week-over-week |
| **Average watch time / % completion** | Hook + retention strength | 60%+ completion = strong; <30% = hook weak |
| **Like rate** (likes / views) | Quality signal | 5%+ = good; 8%+ = strong |
| **Save rate** (saves / views) | Buyer intent signal | 1%+ = strong; >3% = exceptional |
| **Comment rate** | Engagement / controversy | 0.5%+ = engaging |
| **Share rate** | Word-of-mouth signal | 1%+ = strong |

**Saves are the leading indicator of sales.** A high-views/low-saves video is "interesting content"; a moderate-views/high-saves video is "people about to buy." Optimize for saves on product videos.

## The Friday template

Open a text file or spreadsheet. For each of the week's videos, fill in:

```
Date posted: [date]
Product featured: [slug from product-catalog.md]
Hook category: [Symptom / Tradition / POV / Comment / Unboxing]
Structure: [matching name from video-structures.md]
Views: [#]
Completion %: [%]
Like rate: [%]
Save rate: [%]
Comments worth noting: [pull the top 3 most engaged comments]
```

Then write 1-2 sentences answering:

> Which video this week earned the most SAVES per view?
> What was different about its hook / structure / product?
> What should next week's batch include MORE of?

## Patterns to look for

### Pattern 1 — "Save floor by hook category"

Over 3-4 weeks, you'll see different hook categories average different save rates. Example pattern:

```
Symptom-aware hooks: 2.1% save rate (consistent winner for cold traffic)
Tradition explainer: 1.4% save rate (lower volume, higher conversion intent)
POV: 0.6% save rate (high views, low intent — uses CTA placement)
Comment response: 1.8% save rate (engagement multiplier)
Unboxing: 1.2% save rate (good for skeptical audience)
```

Adjust your batching shoot list to lean into the categories that save best for YOUR audience.

### Pattern 2 — "Product save floor"

Different products earn different save floors. Incense vs orgone vs pyramid will diverge. Track per-product over a month:

```
Black love incense — 1.8% avg save rate over 6 videos
Amethyst pyramid — 0.9% avg save rate over 4 videos
Orgone protection — 2.3% avg save rate over 5 videos
```

The pyramid here is probably under-converting — maybe wrong hook category. Re-shoot with a Tradition Explainer next batch.

### Pattern 3 — "Hour-of-day"

Some accounts have a posting window that consistently outperforms. Track post time vs views over 3-4 weeks. Usually 6-9pm local (audience's local) is strong; weekday mornings are weak. Confirm for YOUR audience.

### Pattern 4 — "Topic adjacency"

When you check your Analytics → Followers → "Top videos your followers watched", you'll see what ELSE your audience watches. If they watch a lot of, say, herbalism content — that's a hook angle you can try ("the herbal cousin to my crystal pyramid practice").

## What NOT to chase

❌ **Don't chase a viral video on a non-product topic.** Some accounts get a hit on a personal-story video and try to recreate it. The audience that came for that story isn't going to buy a crystal pyramid. Stay on-niche.

❌ **Don't change strategy after a single bad week.** Algorithm volatility is real. 2-3 weeks of data minimum before adjusting.

❌ **Don't post more frequently because a video did well.** The temptation is "let me post 5 more on this topic this week." See [daily-rhythm.md](daily-rhythm.md) — overposting after a hit dilutes attention. 1-2/day max.

❌ **Don't delete underperforming videos.** They cost you nothing once posted, and they sometimes find an audience weeks later. The "video graveyard" is part of the algorithm's training data on your account.

## When to escalate to Mike

DM Mike if you see:

- A specific product getting consistent buyer-intent signals (saves >3%, comments asking how to buy) — he can confirm stock and consider featured placement on the site.
- A negative review or controversy comment about a product. He'll want to know.
- A video that earned >100K views — he might want to repost or amplify on the site.
- A pattern that suggests a product is being received in a way the catalog doesn't predict (e.g., audience asking "is this safe for cats?" — useful product copy update).

## Friday output

After 30 minutes of analytics review, your output should be:

1. Top performer of the week + WHY (one sentence)
2. Bottom performer + WHY (one sentence)
3. Tomorrow's batch shoot list (5-7 products) — adjusted based on patterns
4. One thing to STOP doing (specific to your account)
5. One thing to START doing (specific to your account)

Not a 5-page report. A 5-line decision document.

## The monthly review (last Friday of the month)

Once a month, zoom out and write 2-3 paragraphs:

- What's the trajectory? (Followers gained, average views per video, etc.)
- What's the top hook category for THIS month?
- What's the top product?
- What's NOT working that I should drop?
- What experiment will I run next month?

This monthly write-up goes into a `tiktok/monthly-reviews/<YYYY-MM>.md` file. Helps you see drift over time and forces you to commit to one experiment per month.
